Small Leaks Become Big Repairs

Most of the water damage and mould calls we see in Guelph started as a leak somebody noticed and put off. A pinhole in a copper line behind drywall can run for months. A toilet flapper that does not seal wastes hundreds of litres a day and shows up on the next water bill. Guelph's hard water also eats away at older copper and galvanized pipe from the inside, so pinholes in 40- to 60-year-old homes are routine rather than rare.

We start by confirming where the water is actually coming from, which is often not where it shows up. Then we fix the pipe properly: cut out the failed section, replace it with new copper or PEX, test it under pressure and, where the wall had to be opened, leave a clean cut that is easy for a drywaller to patch.

Water pouring out right now? Shut off the main valve and see our emergency plumber page. If the leak is at the water heater, go to water heater repair. For costs, see our plumber cost guide.
